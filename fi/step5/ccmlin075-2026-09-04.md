## Standings: single-thread 1-D complex f64 forward

`fft_bench`'s own driver (`src/fft_bench.cpp`, Google Benchmark), filtered per size
(`--benchmark_filter='run_fft<1 << k, 1>$'`, benchmark names use shift notation, not
decimal N), `--benchmark_repetitions=1 --benchmark_format=json`, one JSON run per
(arm, N, round). Arms interleaved `before, after, mkl, after2, before, after, mkl,
after2, ...` across 7 rounds; `after2` reruns the AFTER `admiral_bench` binary a second
time per round as a same-binary control. Reduced by min over the 7 rounds per cell.

| N | before (ns) | after (ns) | mkl (ns) | after2 (ns) | before/mkl | after/mkl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 256  | 212.69  | 184.20  | 185.03  | 186.87  | 1.149 | 0.996 |
| 512  | 472.64  | 427.92  | 371.90  | 427.78  | 1.271 | 1.151 |
| 1024 | 1386.78 | 1040.62 | 979.80  | 1041.36 | 1.415 | 1.062 |
| 2048 | 2839.64 | 2692.67 | 2302.23 | 2671.34 | 1.233 | 1.170 |
| 4096 | 6287.72 | 5810.73 | 5259.06 | 5788.34 | 1.196 | 1.105 |

**Same-binary control floor** (after2/after, both runs of the identical AFTER binary):
0.9921 - 1.0145, i.e. the round-to-round noise floor is about **1.5%**.

## After against before, and why the plan's row is not the baseline

| N | after/before | same-binary control (after2/after) |
|---|---|---|
| 256  | 0.866 | 1.0145 |
| 512  | 0.905 | 0.9997 |
| 1024 | 0.750 | 1.0007 |
| 2048 | 0.948 | 0.9921 |
| 4096 | 0.924 | 0.9961 |

Every size improves beyond the 1.5% floor; 1024 by 25%. The plan's starting row (admiral/MKL
1.35 @256, 1.26 @1024, 1.17 @4096) is a DIFFERENT measurement and is not comparable to any row
above: it is `report/fix4/PHASE1.md`'s ns ratio of `route_driver_factor` on a forced chain against
`mkl_bench` built from the same `fft_bench.cpp` under `intel-oneapi-mkl/2024.2.2` and `gcc/14.2.0`,
where MKL read 153 ns at 256 and 924 ns at 1024. Here MKL is `intel-oneapi-mkl/2026.0.0` under
`gcc/14.3.0` through fft_bench's own driver and reads 185.03 ns and 979.80 ns, and admiral runs its
auto-routed plan, not a forced chain. The before/MKL column of this run (1.149 / 1.415 / 1.196) is
the only baseline the after/MKL column (0.996 / 1.062 / 1.105) can be read against. The 3.3% GHz
spread below caps how far the absolute ns figures can be trusted; the ratios within one round are
tighter than that, which is what the same-binary control measures.

Raw per-round data: `ccmlin075-2026-09-04-raw.tsv` (7 rounds x 5 sizes x 4 arms, produced by
`measure.sh`); the `nthreads=0` single round is `ccmlin075-2026-09-04-auto.tsv`.

## GHz check

`perf stat -e cycles,task-clock`, one run per arm at N=1024, `taskset -c 4`,
`--benchmark_min_time=1s`, two repetitions:

| rep | before | after | mkl |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4.012 GHz | 3.880 GHz | 3.956 GHz |
| 2 | 3.953 GHz | 3.926 GHz | 3.943 GHz |

Spread 3.880-4.012 GHz (3.3%), above the 1% target. The governor is `intel_pstate`
`powersave` with turbo on (`no_turbo=0`); the spread does not track a fixed arm across
the two repetitions (after is lowest in rep 1, middle in rep 2), so this reads as
turbo/thermal jitter rather than a systematic clock bias favoring one arm — but it is
disclosed as a caveat on the absolute ns numbers above, not swept under the same-binary
floor (which is a separate, and tighter, measurement).

## Supplementary: admiral `nthreads=0` (auto), one round, AFTER binary

`admiral_omp_bench` (AFTER build), `taskset -c 4`, one round, no min-reduction:

| N | ns/transform |
|---|---|
| 256  | 234.90 |
| 512  | 527.55 |
| 1024 | 1235.31 |

| 4096 | 6975.86 |

All five sizes are well under the 2^15-element auto-serial threshold, so `nthreads=0`
should match the pinned single-thread numbers above; it reads 10-19% slower instead.
One round with no reduction cannot separate a real `admiral_omp_bench`-build effect
(different TU, OpenMP link) from noise — flagged as a lead for a follow-up sweep, not a
finding.
